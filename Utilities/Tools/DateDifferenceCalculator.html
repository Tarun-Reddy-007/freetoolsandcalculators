About Date Difference Calculations

A date interval can be described in several valid ways: inclusive calendar days, elapsed days between midnight boundaries, business days, or a calendar breakdown into years, months, and days. This calculator shows those perspectives together and can include weekends, holidays, and a milestone percentage so the result fits planning, scheduling, and timeline review.

Always check whether the task counts the start and end dates. Month lengths vary, leap years affect February, and business-day rules depend on the organization and locale. The tool uses the dates and options entered in the browser; for contracts, payroll, legal deadlines, or regulated reporting, confirm the convention with the governing source.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the start and end dates counted?

The calendar-day total uses inclusive counting, so a start date and the same end date represent one calendar day. Review the displayed convention when comparing the result with a system that uses elapsed or end-exclusive days.

How are business days calculated?

The tool counts dates according to the weekend option and removes holidays entered in the supported date format. Local public holidays, partial days, and organization-specific rules may require a separate adjustment.

How are leap years handled?

Calendar arithmetic follows the Gregorian date representation used by the browser, including the extra day in leap years. This can affect totals and the years-months-days breakdown.

What does a milestone percentage represent?

It identifies the date at the selected fraction of the interval between the start and end dates. It is a planning reference and may need adjustment when a project uses working time rather than calendar time.
